## Decision exchange

The primary asked each reviewer to address two concrete choices in one bounded exchange: clarify the routine route before testing versus freeze the current package, and software-first validation versus broader business scope. Each read the peers' initial reports and recorded reasons and reopening conditions in its own report.

The initial product recommendation put simplification after evidence; usability favored making the short path visible while piloting. Their final positions crossed: **Product favored clarification before freezing**, while **Usability revised to freeze first**. QA favored freezing. Statements of agreement in individual reports refer to the earlier text each had read; they are not a final unanimous vote.

As the responsible superior, primary chaired the remaining sequencing decision asynchronously, using those reasons, and sent the decision and dissent back to all three affected reviewers. **Decision: freeze the current package for the first small cohort.** The routine-path concern is plausible, but there is no observed newcomer failure yet. Measuring the current version supplies a diagnosis; a later identified revision can test the remedy. This is the chair's judgment, not majority voting or proof that Product's recommendation is wrong. Reopen a targeted entry-path revision if first-use evidence shows a missed action/companion or omitted required checks.

All three supported a bounded software cohort first and business validation separately. This is a sequence, not removal of the owner's business-management ambition or any of the four skills. Reorder if actual task access or the owner's next consequential decision favors a business workflow. No expert consultation was warranted: the remaining gap is observed task performance, not an unresolved specialist question.

## Outcome and learning

The team recommends measuring **accepted real work, total effort and owner rescue** before adding more operating theory. The [next-proof brief](../northstar/next-proof.md) makes the first comparison, decision rule, separate business follow-up, owners and limitations concrete. It proposes no automatic runtime enforcement or new ceremonial layer.

What worked in this task: scoped independent reviewers delivered different concerns, source inspection kept claims bounded, an actual peer exchange changed opinions, and the chair resolved the remaining choice without requesting routine owner arbitration. This is evidence that those steps happened here, not that three reviewers were cheaper or better than one.

What did not resolve the central question: another document review cannot show better accepted-task economics. Document walkthroughs are not user observations; familiar/synthetic exercises cannot supply held-out results. Repeating this kind of review without a new task observation would leave the same uncertainty. The next assignment must retrieve this decision and run or identify the concrete next proof, rather than restart a generic strategy debate.

Retain the four-skill package, meaningful checkpoint reviews, reasoned dissent, bounded recovery and valid local checks. Defer new ceremony types and unmeasured simplification. Persist the failed inference—cheap worker selection alone proves savings—as ruled out; reopen an efficiency claim only with comparable accepted outcomes and complete accounting. Carry forward successful evidence reuse; reopen a check only for a relevant change, contradiction or coverage gap.
