# Approve the repair before the wrench turns
**Spoke & Sprocket** · Concept for independent bicycle repair shops

---

## A repair stalls while the customer waits
At fictional shop **North Loop Cycles**, a mechanic finds a worn chain and damaged cassette during a tune-up. The customer is away; the estimate sits in a text thread.

`[MISSING IMAGE: shared screen 2 — fictional inspection with two findings, photos marked as placeholders, and estimate]`

---

## Turn findings into a clear choice
The mechanic records **C$48 chain + C$72 cassette + C$35 labor = C$155 CAD**. The customer can approve all, decline, or ask a question from one link. No work is authorized until the customer chooses.

`[MISSING IMAGE: shared screen 3 — fictional itemized estimate and customer decision controls]`

---

## Keep the shop in control
An optional assistant can draft a plain-language explanation from the mechanic’s notes; a person reviews and sends it. If the shop chooses local inference, setup needs a shop-owned compatible computer and model; availability, quality, and data handling must be verified. Other services may still use the internet.

`[MISSING IMAGE: shared screen 4 — mechanic-reviewed draft, edit and send controls, local/cloud setting]`

---

## Make the next step visible
Customer-approved work moves to **Authorized**; declined items stay off the work order. Staff see who decided and when, then update the customer when the bike is ready.

`[MISSING IMAGE: shared screen 5 — fictional repair queue with explicit statuses and audit timestamps]`

---

## Test the workflow with one repair
Proposed pilot: one shop, one shared estimate link, staff-reviewed messages, and an approval history. Price and integrations are open questions. How do you handle estimates today, what tools or alternatives do you use, and who needs to act? What would you change? What would you pay per shop each month, including zero, and in what currency? Hypothetical only; this concept is not working software.
