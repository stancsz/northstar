Northstar's exhausted-recovery rule preserves the original criterion and history, limits an alternative route to one trial, and directs unresolved authority to its actual owner (`skills/northstar/SKILL.md`, especially lines 88–99 and 111–117; `skills/codex-subagents/SKILL.md`, lines 45–72 and 90–107).

The QA skill also distinguishes defects, suggestions and unverified checks (`skills/codex-qa/SKILL.md`, “Challenge the result, then repair”). This is consistent with the dogfood report-only assignment.
- The Advisor contract says unavailable or incomplete advice is a visible failure. The caller raises on HTTP errors, network/timeout errors, missing choices/content, non-stop completion and empty content, then returns an `unavailable` result for its handled error classes (`skills/codex-advisor/SKILL.md`, lines 69–73; `skills/codex-advisor/scripts/ask_expert.py`, lines 99–116 and 147–151). I found no false-success path in these inspected branches. This was static source inspection only; no provider or caller regression was run.
- Prior evaluations generally bound their conclusions to the actual artifacts and trials. The stall trials call their checkpoints synthetic and say they do not prove matched improvement, savings or long-session reliability (`docs/evals/stall-recovery-trials.md`, “Conclusion and limits”). QA's rendered browser case is also explicitly a simple synthetic fixture, not broad delivery quality (`docs/evals/qa-suite.md`, “Conclusion and limitations”). Coordinated-delivery and operating-system evaluations disclose absent long-session, live-business and measured-cost evidence. These caveats support package practice/correctness claims while preventing them from being mistaken for outcome-effectiveness proof.

**Suggestion — next proof:** If the owner wants to claim efficacy or economy, run a small held-out matched task comparison with unchanged acceptance and comparable starting state. Count accepted outcomes, elapsed time, worker/commander/reviewer/advisor/rework usage, and interventions; preserve unknown usage as unknown. The present fixtures are useful feasibility evidence but are authored/synthetic and do not provide an old-skill control. Keep that work as a later, bounded evaluation rather than expanding this review into a new test framework.

**Unverified — effectiveness and integration in real use:** No live task was executed for this review. The existing evidence does not establish that four-skill invocation works across Codex hosts, that guidance transfers to naturally occurring complex stalls/disagreement, that the package improves held-out task outcomes, or that lower-tier/model allocation reduces total cost. The caller's unusual malformed-response shapes outside its handled branches were not exercised; although the inspected exceptions fail visibly, runtime behavior remains unverified. Advisor consultation was not warranted here: the reviewed source and records resolved the scoped questions, and the dogfood goal prohibits calls merely to exercise the skill.

## Peer exchange

I read the product and usability reports after my independent assessment. They agree on the central gap: current evidence does not show better accepted outcomes or lower all-in effort on real tasks. The product report recommends software work first and simplification after a matched pilot; usability recommends making routine routing easier while running that pilot and suggests routine, multi-part and failure/integration examples. This is a material sequencing difference because changing the quick-start during the outcome comparison changes the intervention being evaluated.

**Recommendation to the chair:** freeze the current four-skill package for one small baseline pilot. Include matched software tasks spanning routine, coordination-worthy and failure/recovery conditions where real eligible tasks exist; use the same capable lower-cost worker/model and settings in both Northstar and the named baseline arm. Keep the package's business-operation scope intact, but treat a business workflow as a separate next validation with its own acceptance criteria. Software-only evidence cannot validate business use. After the baseline pilot, run a short first-use routing check or revise the quick start, then freeze that revision before any follow-on comparison. If the chair chooses to clarify routing first, label that revision as the intervention and do not pool its observations with results from the frozen package.

**Minimum falsifiable pilot rule:** before task assignment, pair tasks by type/difficulty, record unchanged acceptance criteria, starting artifacts/state, authorization and available tools, then assign comparable workers/model effort to the two arms. Keep instructions, artifacts and lessons from crossing arms; use fresh contexts or equivalent isolation. Have a reviewer apply the same criteria without being steered by arm where feasible. Count rescue/takeover as an intervention and include commander, worker, reviewer, consultation, repair and rework effort in each task's elapsed-time/token/cost accounting. Missing usage stays unknown and bars a cost/savings conclusion; contamination, unequal starts or changed criteria make that pair non-comparable.

A pilot supports only a next-step decision, not statistical proof: continue a larger validation only if Northstar's accepted outcomes are no worse on the matched tasks and it shows a predeclared benefit in accepted share, owner effort or total effort without material quality/authority failures. A material failure is a stop-and-repair signal. Mixed/no-benefit results mean no efficacy or economy claim; name the unresolved factor and run at most a focused follow-up. Report pair-level outcomes and any rescue or missing data rather than a blended score. The task count and practical threshold should be fixed by the primary before starting, not tuned after seeing results.

This resolves the cross-report method tension provisionally; primary chairs final sequencing and pilot acceptance. It does not alter business scope or establish broad package value.
