# Product delivery source critique

Date: 2026-10-21. Author: independent product/source critic. Status: source review complete; not implementation acceptance. Scope: the working-tree Northstar/QA instructions, goal template, lifecycle and role references, next-proof direction, and finish-contract record. No network, providers, product execution, or skill edits. Existing dirty work was inspected read-only and preserved.

## Judgment

These skills provide a credible delivery discipline. They do **not yet demonstrate that an agent given a real product brief can carry it to usable production or a sellable result**. The harshest defensible criticism is the distance between the ambition and the evidence, not an absent instruction to finish. Repeating that instruction would add little.

Existing safeguards are substantive: [Northstar](../../../skills/northstar/SKILL.md), lines 46–54, requires continuation beyond a first slice and distinguishes an MVP from release/business readiness; lines 107–113 preserve unmet requirements and prohibit silent success redefinition. Lines 141–151 separate authors from independent certification. [QA](../../../skills/codex-qa/SKILL.md), lines 16–39, starts from the request, requires real effects and failures, and preserves unavailable evidence as unverified. The [lifecycle](../../../skills/northstar/references/goal-driven-engineering.md#execute-and-accept) already requires integrated inspection. Preserve these rules.

## Highest-value gaps

### 1. No demonstrated full product delivery

**Type: unproven adherence/effectiveness; not a missing continuation instruction.**

[Next proof](../../northstar/next-proof.md), lines 3, 13 and 17–21, explicitly says the real-task pilot has not run and existing packaging/narrow exercises do not establish practical outcomes. [Finish contract](../../goal/finish-contract/GOAL.md), lines 13–23 and 33–41, establishes scoped archive/text behavior, retains an initial evidence gap, and expressly disclaims real-product completion, sustained autonomy and commercial readiness. Its tested endpoint is a local file artifact, not a customer completing a real integrated service workflow.

**Delivery consequence:** competent wording and tiny cold probes can coexist with failure at the expensive last mile: integration, real access/data, release, adoption, support and recovery. This review cannot say those failures occurred; it can say the current evidence does not exclude them.

**Improvement:** prioritize the existing real-task sequence over more generic policy. In a separately owned product repository, freeze an authorized original outcome that crosses implementation, integration and actual delivery. Retain all unaccepted cases, owner rescues, review/repair work and missing observations. A matched baseline is needed for a benefit claim; one successful real delivery would establish narrower feasibility only.

**Evidence that would change this finding:** a complete original brief, unchanged terminal criteria, traceable revisions/actions, independent actual-environment outcome checks and operating observations, with full interventions/effort accounted for. A final screenshot or deployment receipt alone would not suffice.

### 2. The goal template makes the current slice easier to close than the original request

**Type: instruction/record ambiguity in the initial source-only review; subsequently supplied conversation evidence demonstrates premature closure, without proving this template caused it.**

The [template](../../../skills/northstar/templates/GOAL.md), lines 1, 9–17 and 40–44, has one delivery, one must-pass set, “excluded or later,” and one acceptance section. It says preserve broader commitments, but does not explicitly distinguish the original requested endpoint from the currently accepted increment in the completion record. Northstar's prose supplies that distinction; the template does not make its disposition easy to inspect. The seeded archive cases test a clear known remainder, not whether a commander discovers and preserves omitted obligations from an open product brief.

**Delivery consequence:** “READY for this increment” can be remembered as “request complete” across compressed context or handoff even while each local report is accurate. The initial source-only review could establish only this plausible failure mode. The primary subsequently supplied an actual premature-closure incident from the user conversation; see the qualified incident note below. Its precise cause is not isolated by this review.

**Improvement:** if editing, replace existing outcome/delivery placeholders with one compact distinction: original requested endpoint; current increment; remaining original obligations and next owner/action. Record whether acceptance closes the increment or the full request. Reuse the existing goal, not a second ledger. Do not force production obligations into explicitly local-only work.

**Evidence that would change this finding:** a cold multi-handoff case in which the terminal obligations are derived from the original brief, intermediate work is accepted, context changes, and the owner still reaches or honestly dispositions the complete endpoint without reminder. If current wording succeeds reliably enough for the intended use, retain it rather than patching for reassurance.

### 3. Independent QA is strong locally; final outcome coverage is less explicit

**Type: scope ambiguity plus unproven integrated effectiveness; independence itself is already required.**

[QA](../../../skills/codex-qa/SKILL.md), lines 12, 24–26 and 53–60, sensibly scales to the changed surface/coherent increment and reuses applicable evidence. Original intent and real-entry-point checks are already required in lines 16, 30 and 39. However, the final `READY` is for “current criteria”; neither that decision bullet nor the template's final acceptance explicitly asks whether the accumulated scoped verdicts cover the original requested workflow in its final environment. The lifecycle requires orchestrator integrated inspection, but that owner may also be an author and must rely on a nonauthor verdict for acceptance.

**Delivery consequence:** separately correct component/increment reviews can leave an interaction or release-environment gap. Independence does not make an underscoped review complete.

**Improvement:** bind the existing final review to the original endpoint: the accepting owner must obtain a nonauthor judgment on the integrated promised journey in the intended environment, reusing valid earlier evidence and naming uncovered requirements. This should refine the existing scope/decision sentence, not add a full second QA ceremony or require rerunning every passed test.

**Evidence that would change this finding:** independent inspection of a real multi-increment delivery catches or rules out an integration defect invisible in its component receipts; the final decision explicitly distinguishes reviewed outcome, retained gaps and release authority. An observed failure of the current wording would justify the instruction change more strongly than this walkthrough.

### 4. “Production” and “sellable” need task-specific evidence, not broad quality nouns

**Type: thin practical guidance and unproven commercial/operational results.**

[Northstar](../../../skills/northstar/SKILL.md), lines 47, 52 and 77, correctly names value, the riskiest assumption, security/data integrity and actual-workflow evidence. The [goal reference](../../../skills/northstar/references/goal-driven-engineering.md#define-the-goal) also names payer/sustainability. The [operating practice](../../../skills/northstar/references/operating-system.md#turn-direction-into-todays-work), lines 11–17, requires operating signals, failure response and transfer, and warns that a cadence supplies no scheduler. These are useful obligations; they do not themselves turn a vague “production/sellable” request into discriminating evidence.

**Delivery consequence:** a technically accessible app may still be unusable by a fresh intended user, unsafe with actual data, unrecoverable after a relevant failure, or unsupported as a paid offering. Conversely, imposing enterprise gates on a small authorized local tool would waste effort. Commercial viability cannot be certified by an AI taste review.

**Improvement:** add at most one concise illustrative scenario/reference showing how an explicitly requested production or paid delivery is translated into observable criteria: intended user obtains access and completes value through the real entry point; actual promised integrations/data effects are verified; relevant identity/data/recovery risks are tested; a named operator can detect and handle failure. If the claim is demand or revenue, require corresponding authorized user/transaction evidence and keep unknowns pending. Keep product-specific plans, code and execution records in that product's repository. Prefer an example over a universal deployment checklist.

**Evidence that would change this finding:** an uninvolved intended user completes the promised workflow without builder guidance, relevant recovery is observed, an operator takes an evidenced handoff, and any claimed purchase/adoption/revenue is actually observed over a stated period. No purchase evidence means willingness to pay remains unknown, not that shipping must always stop.

## Dependency and review limits

No unavailable tool/service was demonstrated in this source review. An actual missing provider, credential, release grant, independent reviewer, scheduler or operator would be a specific operational dependency, not proof that the skill lacks a delivery rule. Available tools/access and instructions must be evaluated separately. Markdown cannot itself guarantee persistent execution; that limit is already disclosed, not fixed by adding an application/runtime here.

On source evidence alone, the next action was an evidence-backed product trial and, only where useful, the small endpoint/QA wording refinements above. The subsequently supplied incident warrants a focused closure repair and replay before broader claims. This report is a critic's recommendation. It does not accept new instructions, certify an app, establish a skill success rate, or prove commercial readiness.
