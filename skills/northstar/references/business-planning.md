# Business planning and adversarial research

Use this reference for consequential business plans, market or segment choices, go-to-market and pricing, operating models, financing choices, or scenario analysis where a shallow SWOT would miss decision-changing assumptions. Keep the work proportional: answer the requested decision in an existing brief or record, and load current sources only where facts can change the recommendation. Do not turn ordinary advice into mandatory review, delegation, council, outreach or approval.

## Start from the actual decision

Recover the user's goal, chosen strategy, constraints, resources, commitments and prior decisions from the conversation and project records. Treat an explicitly chosen direction as a constraint to optimize within, not an invitation to restart generic validation. Name genuinely unresolved decisions separately. Distinguish sourced facts, user-reported assets or experience, hypotheses, inferences and unknowns; do not promote inference into fact or reopen a settled choice without changed evidence.

State who must choose and why now. Identify relevant users, beneficiaries, economic buyers, procurement, IT/security, operations, channel partners, investors or capital partners; combine roles when one person holds several. Compare the strongest substitute or status quo, including doing nothing, and explain why a buyer would select it over the proposal. Address switching costs, buying authority, budget source, timing, implementation burden and proof required where they affect the decision.

## Build an evidence-backed case

For current or time-sensitive claims, research primary and authoritative sources first: enacted law and official procurement/funding terms for rules; company and product materials for capabilities and prices; filings, contracts, budgets, purchase records or buyer statements for commercial behavior; and direct customer evidence when authorized. Give source date, scope and applicability. Separate policy alignment from funding eligibility or backing, regulation from willingness to pay, and supplier investment from orders by the specific downstream buyers. Treat vendor claims and forecasts as claims, not observed outcomes. Preserve conflicting evidence and explain remaining uncertainty. Never fabricate probabilities, market size, commitments or financial precision.

Map each material vulnerability with enough information to act:

- The strongest fair version of the buyer's refusal or failure case.
- Supporting evidence, missing evidence and its applicability.
- Consequence and a likelihood rationale grounded in evidence or clearly labeled judgment.
- An observable trigger or leading indicator.
- A mitigation with an owner, direct and opportunity cost, dependencies, and new risks it introduces.
- A practical acceptance or falsification test and decision threshold.
- Residual exposure and a continue, pivot, pause or stop consequence.

Keep claims at their actual maturity: designed, implemented, tested in an authorized environment, independently reviewed, or customer-validated. A proposal is not an implementation; a tabletop thought experiment is not an operational soak, customer test or independent review.

## Adversarial rounds

Use multiple rounds only while the next round can reveal a consequential failure mode, change a mitigation or alter the decision. A useful pattern is:

1. Steelman the buyer's refusal, status quo, and commercial, technical, operational, financing and legal failure cases relevant to the plan. Include a premortem: assume the plan failed and identify the plausible path.
2. Propose concrete responses and a feasible initial plan. Then attack each response: what does it cost, delay, depend on, make harder, or newly expose?
3. Revise and debate the strongest remaining objections. Consider normal operating conditions, adverse conditions and explicit exit conditions.
4. Adjudicate each proposal as retain, simplify, replace or drop, with a reason and residual risk. Rank plausible scenarios by evidence and stated assumptions, not invented confidence percentages.

Pair proposals with counterarguments and resolve them with evidence, explicit assumptions and consequences. Do not let a facilitator's preferred plan win by default. Stop when consequential new findings diminish and the residual risks, decision triggers and next useful evidence are clear. Missing evidence remains unknown or blocks the relevant claim; repetition never turns it green.

## Market, operations and economics

Choose a specific buyer/segment and reachable channel before estimating opportunity. Explain the job, alternative, buying path, sales cycle, qualification and evidence for access. Use market-size estimates only when their source, date, segment, assumptions and bottom-up calculation are defensible; otherwise use a bounded serviceable cohort or customer-level model and state the limit. Treat interviews, letters of intent, pilots and paid orders as different evidence.

Model base, downside and upside scenarios as conditional cases, not predictions. Show the assumptions that drive the recommendation and compare sensitivities or breakeven points. Include relevant fixed and variable costs, acquisition and sales-cycle costs, founder labor, utilization, delivery capacity, support, financing/depreciation, cash collection delays and working capital. Separate booked, recognized and collected revenue where relevant. Do not count the same capacity or revenue twice, assume founder time is free, or imply false precision. Say what changes first in the downside case and when cash, capacity or risk limits force action.

Recommend one feasible default configuration and the strongest alternatives with conditions that would trigger each. Fit it to the user's real team, cash, time, skills, relationships and risk tolerance. Preserve the stated strategic aspiration (for example, a partner/operator model) unless evidence shows why it fails; do not silently replace it with a simpler but different business. A lean consulting offer, product, reseller or managed service can be an entry route or alternative, but explain how it supports or abandons the chosen direction.

Specify the minimum operating model and architecture needed to deliver safely: decision rights, customer responsibilities, security/data boundaries, service levels, human professional implementation/training/handoff, support and exit. Treat customer-managed/handoff and ongoing managed support as distinct choices with distinct workload and economics. Preserve customer autonomy and avoid lock-in by default. Make delivery, maintenance and incident ownership explicit; a diagram or plan alone does not prove operations work.

For public-sector or regulated markets, separately label strategic alignment, enacted obligations, procurement requirements, available programs, eligibility and actual qualification. Cite the exact program or rule and conditions. Do not infer a grant, contract, investment or endorsement from broad policy language.

## Decision-ready handoff

Deliver the recommendation, not just a list of questions. Include the selected plan and why it is robust under stated assumptions; buyer and alternative; concise evidence and key unknowns; scenario/economic comparison; leading risks with owner, mitigation, cost and residual exposure; phased deliverables and acceptance criteria; normal/adverse/exit triggers; and the next highest-value evidence or authorized action. Mark which choices are decided, assumed or still owner-retained. State what the evidence does not establish.

Do not promise signings, profit, a particular market outcome, MBA accreditation, external validation or independent review when the same author did the work. Advice and simulated adversarial rounds do not authorize customer contact, purchases, applications, deployments or sensitive-data processing. Use an available, authorized reviewer, council or specialist only when it could materially change the decision; disclose unavailable routing and never imply it succeeded or silently switch providers.
